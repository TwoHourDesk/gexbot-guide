# Named reads (H1–H15)

**Which Gexbot capability this is.** None new. This chapter is a drill sheet: fifteen numbered reads so the reader can produce each combination without inventing a sixteenth. Classic already marked the strikes whose gamma map just redrew — **max-change**. Gexbot can also ping you when the index arrives at a named node. That ping is timing. It is not a sixteenth heuristic.

**What you will be able to do.** Walk fifteen reads — this book numbers them H1 through H15 — each with a pressure word and a hedge-chain link. The first five are the core. The next ten sit on Gexbot screens the first five leave unused, and two of them (H12, H13) are prerequisites the first five quietly assume. Two product features sit under those drills and do not get a number: the ping, and max-change. Not a system. No hit rate.

**What you already need.** Chapters 1–7. If a stop or target is not in the docs, it is **inferred**.

A **heuristic** in this book is a **naming drill**: practice saying what you are looking at. It is not an order to send.

**Default clocks.** Residual: 09:30 ET–16:00. Early regular-hours map to ES or NQ: 09:29–11:00. Last-hour family: 15:00–16:00. Do not pool them.

**Stand down** when Orderflow is two-sided noise, leftover is too thin to name a major, you are using the falling-vol map on a rising-vol tape, you used full-book zero gamma for a 0DTE scalp, or you treated converted walls as exchange volume. Those five look different. Each is a case where the screen cannot supply the owner, the regime, or the object the read needs, and a read without them is a guess with a heuristic number on it.

## H1 — SPX GEX-orderflow pivot (*folklore*)

This drill sits **GEX orderflow** on SPX. Hedge-chain link 1 (the print), plus link 4 if you add convexity.

Pressure is **mandate** on absorb. Owner still needs the 2×2. Classic cannot supply it.

The read: a standout plus GEX-orderflow bar into a call-heavy node is a **local-top** *candidate* on SPX. The mirror minus bar is a local-bottom candidate. The read is off when the docs’ “SPX trading like SPY” (VIX above 20) *folklore* applies, or when convexity shows the bar was **long calls**.

Forced-flow sentence: dealer absorbed call-sided leftover · mandate · print · *unknown direction until the 2×2* · ES.

The misread: fading a standout plus bar without checking convexity. If the bar was long calls, the owner wants expansion and the dealer on the mirror chases; the reader is short into the one case the folklore does not cover.

## H2 — Name the plus-DEX (expansion versus pin)

This drill pairs **DEX orderflow** with **convexity orderflow**. Link 1, then the sign of link 4.

Pressure: mandate on the dealer mirror; customer incentive differs by cell.

The read: plus DEX and plus convexity → **long call** (expansion). Plus DEX and minus convexity → **short put** (pin). Mirror the signs for minus DEX. Name the 2×2 cell or do not name the print.

<TermFirst id="h2" />

## H3 — Fear tape versus grind tape

This drill sits a **sequence** of convexity-orderflow bars, plus the nets. It is a **day-type** filter. It is not an entry.

Pressure: aggregate pending gamma lean (mandate) plus customer vol bid or offer (incentive).

The read: many small plus-convexity bars → fear / vol-bid. Many small minus-convexity bars → grind / premium-sale (*folklore*). Do not name the day at 09:35 from one spike.

<TermFirst id="h3" />

## H4 — Walk into a stack

This drill sits State: options profile, DEX, and convexity. Link 4 and customer incentive.

Pressure: customer **incentive** (vol regime). Dealer **mandate** on the mirror. Apply the vol flip from Chapter 4.

The read: in falling vol, approach a customer-long node → stall *candidate*; approach a customer-short node → continuation *candidate*. Do not use a stack that printed *after* the touch — that is a labeling leak.

Walls are hypotheses. Chapter 9 will test them with **acceptance through** the node.

<TermFirst id="h4" />

## H5 — Last-hour 0DTE short-convexity magnet

This drill sits the clock family from Chapter 7. Hedge-chain **link 5**, not link 4. Beta feature: residual-only, still being practiced.

Pressure: dealer **mandate**, clock or vol, residual-only. Off if net minus-vanna is not “extreme” by the docs’ discretionary $800 million / $1000 million SPX note (*folklore*).

The read: pin or attract toward 0DTE minus-convexity. Different family from H1–H4. Flow dies if the node goes at-the-money.

<TermFirst id="h5" />

## More reads on the same screens

H1 through H5 leave several Gexbot capabilities untouched: the two implied-vol dots on the options profile, the lookback slider, the switch between expiry groups, net GEX, the call-versus-put split of aggregate DEX, the *shape* of the convexity ladder, and the points where the minus-vanna and charm ladders cross zero. The ten drills below sit on those. Every one keeps the same discipline: a pressure word, a hedge-chain link, a label, and a misread.

Two of them come first in practice. H12 tells you how to tag the vol regime on the screen, which H4, H6, and H7 need. H13 tells you when a Classic pile has no leftover behind it, which every State read needs.

## H6 — Crowded short-convexity shove (*folklore*)

This drill sits the **shape** of the convexity ladder, not one node. Link 4 for the dealer; customer incentive for the fuel.

Customer-short gamma can be spread across many strikes, or bunched at one or two strikes just under or over the index. This book calls the first **well-distributed** and the second **crowded**. The docs describe well-distributed minus-convexity as a liquid, premium-selling tape, and crowded minus-convexity near the index as their favorite SPY pattern: “one shove” through it.

Pressure: the customers who wrote that gamma are **incentivized** to defend the strike, and then to cover if the index takes it. The dealer on the mirror is long gamma there and fades, so the fuel comes from the customer channel, not from the dealer. That is Gexbot’s distinctive claim, and the reason a dealer-only map does not show this pattern.

Distinct from H4: H4 reads the *approach* to a node; H6 reads how *concentrated* the short gamma is. The vol flip still applies. In rising vol, shorts defend and the crowd can hold.

The misread: treating every minus-convexity node as a magnet. A reader who does that expects a pin at a crowded node in falling vol and is on the wrong side when the shorts cover through it. Concentration and vol regime decide whether the node is a magnet or a trapdoor; the convexity ladder shows only the first, and H12 supplies the second.

## H7 — Transition zone as target (*folklore*)

This drill sits the DEX ladder and the convexity ladder where the **sign changes**. Link 4; the sign of the pending re-hedge flips there.

Where heavy customer-short bars give way to fresh customer-long bars, or where minus-convexity gives way to plus-convexity, this book calls a **transition zone**. The docs call the DEX version a target or reversion zone, and the convexity version a pivot where incentives reshuffle.

This is the missing half of H4. H4 names the node you approach; H7 names where the move is likely to lose its pressure. Pressure: customer **incentive** changes sign at the transition; dealer **mandate** flips from chase to fade or the reverse.

The misread: aiming at the tallest bar instead of the sign change. A reader who does that holds for a target pressure never promised; the tallest bar is where pressure is greatest, not where it ends.

## H8 — Net GEX: squeeze versus single-bar reversion (*folklore*)

This drill sits **net GEX** read together with the distribution of the GEX profile and with **net convexity**. It is the running-integral read of GEX, the way H3 is the sequence read of convexity.

A **squeeze** is a move that feeds itself because dealers short gamma on that side must chase it (*market-general*). The docs’ read: when upside convexity is high and rising and the bars above the index are roughly **equal**, look for a squeeze. When one bar above the index **predominates**, look for reversion at that bar. Mirror both for the downside.

Read net GEX with net convexity before naming either. Net GEX says the gamma is call-sided or put-sided; net convexity says whether it was bought or sold. Same sign, different owner, different pressure.

Pressure: the net is the day’s aggregate lean of link 4, the pending gamma re-hedge. Distinct from H1, which is one bar, and H3, which is a convexity sequence.

The misread: reading high net GEX as bullish. A reader who does that buys a call-heavy day that may be sold calls; the net says where the curvature is, not which way price goes, and net convexity says who owns it.

## H9 — Aggregate DEX: the SPX baseline and the SPY divergence (*folklore*)

This drill sits the call-versus-put split of **aggregate DEX**. It is a record of leans (link 3, already hedged), so it names day character, not pending flow.

The docs describe a normal state for SPX in low vol: during an uptrend, put aggregate DEX is plus, call aggregate DEX is minus, and the net is mildly minus. That is the hedge-and-overwrite machine at work: customers buying puts and selling calls. Negative call aggregate DEX with positive put aggregate DEX marks a broad short-vol day. This book calls that normal state the **SPX baseline**. The drill is to know the baseline and name the deviation from it.

On SPY the docs call a **divergence** between price and its own aggregate DEX “particularly powerful”: price makes a new high while aggregate DEX does not, or the reverse. The docs add that when VIX is above 20, SPX starts trading like SPY.

The misread: reading a minus net aggregate DEX on SPX as bearish. A reader who does that shorts the baseline; in quiet tape that sign is the normal state, and the deviation from it is the read.

## H10 — Wall built versus wall inherited (docs feature; read *inferred*)

This drill sits the **lookback dots** and **history slider** on Classic and State, plus Classic’s **max-change** strikes — the shelves whose gamma map changed the most over the last 1, 5, 15, or 30 minutes. On Classic the pressure word is **unknown**. On State the direction the leftover grew tells you whether customers added or covered.

A node that built into price through the morning is a different object from one already there at 09:31. The slider also shows you what was knowable at the moment of a touch, which is the labeling-leak rule made visible on the screen.

Max-change marks where the map just redrew. Attention, location only, unsigned. Pair it with State to name the owner. Alone it is not a read. It does not get its own H-number.

The misread: reading a lookback dot as a future target, or a max-change strike as a reversal. Both are where the map was or where it moved; a reader who trades them has attached a direction to a location that carries none.

<TermFirst id="h10" />

## H11 — Which expiry group is live (docs)

This drill sits the switch between **latest**, **next**, and **full**. Every other drill in this chapter assumes latest. This one makes the choice explicit.

Sit latest by default. Switch to next when latest is dead or too thin to name a major, or when you are studying the roll. Use full only as overnight context. The stand-down for “full-book zero gamma on a 0DTE scalp” lives here.

The misread: mixing expiry groups inside one sentence. A latest DEX node and a full zero gamma are not the same object, and a reader who pairs them has built a level out of two clocks.

<TermFirst id="h11" />

## H12 — Tag the vol regime from the skew dots (docs feature; rules *inferred*)

This drill sits the two implied-vol dots on the **options profile**. H4, H6, and H7 all tell you to apply the vol flip. This is where the screen tells you which regime you are in.

The options profile draws, on a second axis, the implied vol of the put and the implied vol of the call at each strike for the nearest expiry. This book calls those the **skew dots**. Watch them across lookbacks. If they drift lower through the morning, implied vol is crushing: falling vol. If they lift, rising vol. If the put dots and the call dots disagree, or the drift is flat, stand down on any wall-versus-fuel name.

Two cautions, both *inferred*. Near expiry, 0DTE implied vol is noisy and a late-day drop is mostly the clock, not a regime change. And the dots are a state, not a forecast; a regime can flip after you tag it.

The misread: inferring the vol regime from price direction. Price can rise with vol rising, and a reader who assumes otherwise runs the falling-vol map on the tape it is most wrong for.

## H13 — Classic versus State disagreement (*inferred*)

This drill sits two plans against each other. Classic shows where gamma traded; State shows what is left over after buys and sells cancel. When they disagree, the disagreement is the read.

A large Classic volume pile with a thin State leftover at the same strike means the trading was two-sided and cancelled. Pressure: **none**. Stand down on that strike. A large Classic pile with a customer-short leftover on State is a fuel candidate in falling vol, not support. A large Classic pile with a customer-long leftover is a wall candidate in falling vol. The Classic bar looked the same in all three cases.

This is the practical form of the Chapter 3 gate: a Classic wall is not a State long. It is the only drill that needs two plans.

The misread: reading a quiet State ladder as “nothing traded,” or a balanced Classic node as an empty one. In both, the trading was there and cancelled; a reader who fades the Classic pile is fading a wall nobody is holding.

## H14 — Flush or bid at a low: what you cannot name (*folklore*)

This drill sits **DEX orderflow** and is a deliberate negative. It is the limit case of H2.

A holder forced out of a position is a **flush**. The docs say a local bottom can be an aggressive plus-DEX buyer or a minus-DEX flush as a long is forced out. Now add the open-versus-close limit from Chapter 3: closing a long call and opening a short call both print minus DEX and minus convexity. The screen cannot separate them.

Pressure: the dealer’s **mandate** is the same either way, because their delta moved the same way. So the hedge inference survives. The customer’s **incentive** is unknowable, so the intent inference does not. Write the forced-flow sentence with the hedge and leave the intent blank.

The misread: reading every minus-DEX spike into a low as a fresh bearish bet. A reader who does that shorts a flush — longs being forced out — and is caught when the forcing stops; the screen cannot separate the two.

## H15 — Local zero on the minus-vanna and charm ladders (*folklore*)

This drill sits the **minus-vanna** and **charm** ladders from Chapter 7. Hedge-chain link 5. Same clock family as H5, same beta caveat, same residual-only limit.

The docs say the sign on these ladders flips as the index crosses a level and is zero when the index sits on it. Where the ladder crosses zero, this book calls a **local zero**. The docs call local zeros sharp pivots. H5 reads the net as a magnet toward a node; H15 reads a level on the ladder where passive buying turns into passive selling or the reverse.

Pressure: dealer **mandate**, driven by clock and vol, not by the index moving. Off before the last hour. Off if net minus-vanna and charm are near zero, when the docs say price wanders.

The misread: using a local zero at 10:00 as a level. It is a late-day object; a reader who trades it in the morning has leaked a clock family and is holding a pivot with no flow behind it.

## An alert is a touch

Gexbot can ping you when the index arrives at a named node. This book calls that an **alert**. Classic pings on major positive and major negative. State pings on major call and major put, and on major long and major short. Orderflow pings when a bar passes a size you set.

An alert is a **touch**, not a signal. It is the timing for H4, H6, and H7: the index has arrived at the node whose owner, pressure, and regime you must already have named. If you have not named them, the ping is noise with a sound.

## Order to learn

1. **H12, H13** — prerequisites. Tag the regime; check the leftover behind the pile.
2. **H2, H4, H7** — name the print, name the node, name where pressure ends.
3. **H3, H8, H9** — day character: convexity sequence, GEX integral, DEX baseline.
4. **H1, H6** — the distinctive SPX and SPY folklore. Labels in the heading.
5. **H10, H11, H14** — hygiene: built versus inherited, expiry group, what you cannot name.
6. **H5, H15** — the last-hour family. Different clock. Beta.

## Summary

H12 and H13 are not extra drills. They are the conditions under which H1–H11 may speak. An alert is a third object: a clock with no name. Stand down when the clock family, the vol regime, the expiry group, or the leftover thickness does not support a name — the number on the drill does not survive those four.

## Newcomer questions

1. **Which of these is the one I trade?** None, as a system. H3 has no entry at all.
2. **Can I use H5 at the open?** No. Different clock family.
3. **Does H1 work on NQ?** Only if you rename the claim. It is SPX folklore.
4. **How do I know which vol regime I am in?** H12: watch the skew dots across lookbacks. Not from price direction.
5. **Classic shows a huge wall. Why is State empty there?** H13: the trading was two-sided and cancelled. Pressure none. Stand down on that strike.
6. **An alert just fired. Is that my entry?** No. It is a touch. You should already have named the owner, pressure, and regime.
