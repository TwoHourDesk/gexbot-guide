# Clocks and late greeks

**Which Gexbot capability this is.** Windows that are not allowed to borrow each other’s claims, and two late pictures that move even if the index sits still.

**What you will be able to do.** Pick a legal claim for a clock window, and read last-hour leftover as clock-forced re-hedge — hedge-chain link 5 — with the index unchanged.

**What you already need.** Hedge-chain links 5 and 6 from [Chapter 3](/docs/plans/03-classification), and the Nasdaq family plus conversion from [Chapter 6](/docs/layer/06-nq-es-layer). Do not start here for a morning tape.

## Clock families

A **clock family** is a window with its own legal claim. This book does not pool families.

- **Early regular hours** (about 09:29–11:00 Eastern): the leftover map is still being born. You may watch location. You may not treat a so-far major as if it had existed at the open.
- **Midday residual** (cash regular hours, 09:30–16:00): State and Orderflow as you already sit them.
- **Last-hour magnet** (about 15:00–16:00): time itself becomes the trigger. That is this chapter.
- **Knowability**: you need enough leftover to *name* a major. That is usually after the open auction, not at 09:30:01. A so-far map keeps rewriting.

## Three schoolbook words the late pictures need

**Vega** is how much the option’s value moves if implied volatility moves one point. Gexbot has no standalone vega tape on the retail docs. You need the word because the next two sensitivities are “delta changed because vol changed” and “delta changed because the clock ran.”

**Vanna** is how delta changes when implied volatility changes.

**Charm** is how delta changes as calendar time passes, with the index held still.

Those are schoolbook names. Gexbot then applies them only to **today’s leftover**, not to last night’s whole inventory vault. This book calls that limit **residual-only**.

## −vanna and charm ladders (beta)

Gexbot plots **minus-vanna** as the dollar-delta impact of a *total* implied-vol collapse, from today’s classified leftover only.

It plots **charm** as the dollar-delta impact *per hour* of time, from the same leftover.

Gexbot marks both as **beta**: applied to today’s leftover only, still being practiced, not the full inventory vault. Treat a morning minus-vanna bar as a last-hour object and you have leaked families.

## How the contract dies (*market-general*)

Hedge-chain **link 6** is resolution: the contract expires.

[Chapter 6](/docs/layer/06-nq-es-layer) already named the Nasdaq pair: NDX options are European and cash-settled; QQQ options are American and physically settled.

The S&P pair is the same shape. **SPX** options are European and cash-settled. Daily SPX expiries settle in the afternoon (**PM settlement**). **SPY** options are American and physically settled.

Do not copy an SPX 0DTE resolution story onto SPY — or an NDX story onto QQQ — without saying so. Gexbot shows none of link 6 directly.

## Running example at 15:30

The index has drifted away. The 500 calls are out of the money. Charm drains their delta.

**Bought branch.** The customer is getting shorter (an OTM long call dies). The dealer’s long-ES hedge is now too big, so the dealer **sells** (bar left, passive selling). Mandate, clock trigger, ES.

**Sold branch.** Mirror: the dealer **buys** (bar right).

Flow **dies** if the index walks back to the strike and the calls go at-the-money again (*docs*). A last-hour magnet is not a morning wall with a clock pasted on.

## Summary

Do not pool clock families. Last hour is not the open. Minus-vanna and charm are beta and residual-only. Settlement styles differ; Gexbot does not show resolution.

## Newcomer questions

1. **Is last-hour pinning the same as a morning wall?** No. Different family, different greek, different trigger (clock versus index path).
2. **Must dealers buy if net minus-vanna is $800 million?** Folklore. Discretionary. Residual-only. Beta.
3. **Does Gexbot show expiry settlement hedges?** Not directly. Link 6 is not on a screen.
