# The vol you paid

Your call’s mark can fall while the index stands still, because a number you did not choose — the market’s guess about how much the index will move — fell after you bought. Chapter 4 taught that number as a regime other people trade in. This page is the same number from your seat, where it is a price.

**What this page assumes.** [Chapter 4](/docs/plans/04-state): implied vol, the vol regime, walls and fuel as folklore, DEX as a record, convexity as pending and path-conditional. [The mark](/docs/on-ramp/02-classic) and [your counterparty](/docs/on-ramp/03-classification). **What you will be able to do.** Say what regime you paid in and what it does to the mark; find your own position on each State ladder; say what State tells you about it and what it cannot.

## The regime as a price

When you paid 13 points for the 6000 call, part of that price was a guess about movement: the implied vol at that moment. Chapter 4 said implied vol is the market’s standing guess. From your seat it is an ingredient you paid for. This book calls the implied vol at which you opened a position the **vol paid**.

<TermFirst id="vol-paid" />

If implied vol falls after you buy, the mark falls with the index unchanged; you paid for movement the market has since decided is less likely. If it rises, the mark rises the same way. Chapter 4’s falling-vol and rising-vol regimes are, for you, the difference between paying a fair rent and paying a peak one. The implied vol Chapter 4 showed per strike on the options profile is where you read the regime; the vol paid is what you compare it to.

## Your bar on the ladders

Your 500 bought calls sit on State as a customer-long bar at 6000 on the options profile, a plus bar on the DEX ladder, and a plus bar on the convexity ladder. You own the pile Chapter 4 described from outside.

Chapter 4 also carried the folklore about that pile. In falling vol, a holder of leftover longs has an incentive to sell on approach, and the pile reads as a wall; in rising vol the holder has an incentive to sit, and the pile reads as fuel. From your seat the folklore is not a rule. It is a description of an incentive you will feel: a bought call in falling vol loses value every minute the index does not move, and the pull to sell it into a rally is exactly the pull the wall story is built on. The label stays **folklore**. Feeling the incentive does not make it a mechanism.

## What State tells you, and cannot

State tells you whether other holders share your sign at your strike — whether you are crowded or alone — and it tells you, through DEX, that your counterparty’s first hedge is a record: already done. It cannot tell you your debit, your vol paid, or whether you are ahead. Two holders of the same bar can be in opposite P&L because they paid different vol. The ladder is honest about the pile and silent about the tickets.

## The one thing that changes

A futures position never changes value while price is still. Yours does, on a number that moves for its own reasons and that you cannot see on your chart. Chapter 12’s Gap 2 is the full account of that number as a surface; Chapter 7 gives you its clock.

## Summary

Two holders of the same plus-convexity bar can have opposite marks, because vol paid is a ticket number and the ladder is a pile number. The falling-vol wall folklore is an incentive those two holders will not share: the one who paid 20 and now sees 15 feels the pull to sell; the one who paid 12 does not. The ladder cannot split them. The folklore stays folklore.

## Newcomer questions

1. **I bought calls, the index rose, and I am losing. How?** Vol paid was higher than vol now, or rent ran, or both. Chapter 12 splits the pieces.
2. **If I am part of the wall, is the wall real?** The wall is a hypothesis about people like you in falling vol, labeled folklore. You being one of them does not promote it to fact.
3. **You paid 20 vol; the holder next to you paid 12; both are long the same bar; implied vol is now 15.** Who sits in the wall folklore’s “losing, so sell” seat? Only you. They are ahead on vol. Same bar, opposite incentive. State still draws one pile.
