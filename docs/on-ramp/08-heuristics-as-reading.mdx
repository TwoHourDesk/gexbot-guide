# When you are the level

Every heuristic in Chapter 8 describes what a crowd at a strike is likely to do. If you hold that strike, it describes you. In futures a level is something outside you; here you can be it.

**What this page assumes.** [Chapter 8](/docs/practice/08-heuristics-as-reading): H1–H15 as naming drills, the stand-down conditions, and the rule that a heuristic is a read and never an entry. [The clock on your ticket](/docs/on-ramp/07-clocks-and-late-greeks). **What you will be able to do.** Read four heuristics from the holder’s seat, descriptively, and recognize when a read is about you.

This page describes. Nothing on it tells you to open, close, or hold anything, because the heuristics do not tell anyone that, and moving into the customer seat does not change what they are.

## Own strike

The strike you hold a position at is your **own strike**. At it, a heuristic stops being about a crowd and becomes about you, and the incentive it names is the incentive you will feel.

## Four heuristics from inside

**H4, the wall.** A customer-long pile in falling vol reads as a wall because its holders have an incentive to sell on approach (*folklore*). If your 500 calls are that pile, you are the holder the heuristic is talking about, and the pull to sell into the rally as rent runs and vol falls is the incentive it describes. The read does not say you will. It says holders like you tend to.

**H6, crowded short convexity.** A crowded short-convexity node is a shove waiting for a break: when price takes it, the dealers on the mirror must chase through it. If you sold the calls at that node, you are the one being shoved. The chase is not aimed at you, and it does not care about you; it is your counterparty’s mandate, and your position is on the wrong side of it.

**H5, the last-hour magnet.** Minus-vanna and charm ladders mark where the clock re-hedge will land. If you hold into 15:30 at a marked strike, you are among the holders whose drift the crowd of counterparties is hedging, and the flow that pins or unwinds is partly a response to positions like yours.

**H12, the vol regime.** For a reader, the regime is a tag. For you it is the vol paid: whether you bought at a peak rent or a fair one. The heuristic that tells a reader which map to use tells you what you are paying.

## What the heuristics tell you, and cannot

They tell you what the crowd at your own strike is likely to do, with the same labels and the same stand-down conditions as for any reader. They cannot tell you what you should do, whether you are ahead, or whether the crowd includes you at all; your bar is one contract in a pile. The stand-down applies to you exactly as it applies to a reader: two-sided noise, thin leftover, or the wrong vol map means no read, and a holder with no read has a position and no thesis.

## The one thing that changes

For a futures trader a level is external: price approaches it, holds or breaks, and the trader watches. For a holder the level may be their own position, and the crowd’s incentive at it is their own. Chapter 12’s Stage 6 is where the heuristics return with a job for a holder — choosing the *type* of structure after a thesis exists — and that is the only sense in which they ever become more than reads.

## Summary

The same leftover can be a wall, a shove, a magnet, and a rent bill at once, depending on which heuristic is looking. All four can be true of one holder. None of them is an instruction. The stand-down that silences a reader at a thin or two-sided strike silences the holder there too: a position with no read is a position with no thesis.

## Newcomer questions

1. **H4 and H6 fire at your strike on the same minute.** What is each describing about you, and do they agree on what you will do? H4: your incentive to sell into a rally if you are long and vol is falling (*folklore*). H6: your counterparty’s mandate to chase if you are short and the node breaks. They describe different seats. They do not agree on an action, and neither one is yours to take.
2. **Does H6 tell me to close a short at a crowded node?** No heuristic tells anyone to do anything. It says what the dealers on the mirror must do if price breaks.
3. **Is there a heuristic for my own P&L?** No. Nothing on Gexbot knows what you paid.
