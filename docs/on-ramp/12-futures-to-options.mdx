# The first week of Stage 1

Chapter 12 gives gates, not dates. This page is what the first week toward the first gate looks like, hour by hour, so that the ramp starts on a Monday rather than staying a roadmap.

**What this page assumes.** [Chapter 12](/docs/practice/12-futures-to-options): greek attribution, the position sentence, the eight gaps, and Stage 1’s gate. [The paper position](/docs/on-ramp/11-further-learning). **What you will be able to do.** Run the daily loop of Stage 1 and know what its gate is measuring.

## The hand price

Stage 1’s gate is pricing the ATM option in your head, inside the bid–ask most days. The price worked out from index level, time to expiry, and implied vol before you look at the chain is the **hand price**.

<TermFirst id="hand-price" />

Chapter 11 gave you the shortcut: expected move from the square root of time, straddle at about 0.8 of it, ATM call at about 0.4. Chapter 12’s Gap 1 asks you to know why the shortcut works. The hand price is where knowing and doing meet each morning.

## The daily loop

**Before the open.** Write the morning card as Chapter 11 taught it, with the two lines Chapter 12 added: implied-vol level against its recent range, and the term structure’s slope. Write the hand price for the ATM call and the ATM straddle. Then open the chain and write the market’s bid and ask beside yours. The difference is today’s error; keep it.

**At the open.** Write the paper position from the on-ramp before this one, and write its position sentence in Chapter 12’s form: structure, greeks now, 2×2 cells, thesis, regime paid, what kills it in price, vol, and clock, max loss, exit clock. The paper position now has all three falsifiers and a name.

**At the close.** Attribute the day with Chapter 12’s table: delta, gamma, theta, vega, and the residual. Compare the sum to mark minus debit. Write which falsifier, if any, would have fired, and when.

**End of week.** Five hand prices with five errors; five position sentences; five attributions. The gate asks whether the hand price sat inside the bid–ask most days and whether the attribution left a residual you can name. If not, the second week is the same loop.

## What the on-ramps were

Twelve pages, one seat change each: the map that cannot name you, the ticket with rent on it, the counterparty who is obliged, the vol you paid, the four positions, the contract you would hold, the clock on your ticket, the level that is you, the third falsifier, the untested opening price, the strike that structure describes, the misread with money in it. Chapter 12 said it in one line: you were reading the print, and now you are it. The first week is where that line stops being a sentence.

## Summary

The hand price is the ATM option priced in your head before the chain is opened. Stage 1’s week is a loop: hand price and error before the open, paper position and position sentence at the open, attribution and falsifier check at the close. The gate is a hand price inside the bid–ask most days and a nameable residual.

## Newcomer questions

1. **What if my hand price is outside the bid–ask every day?** Then Gap 1 is open, and the week repeats with Chapter 12’s Gap 1 reading beside it. The gate is not a deadline.
2. **Do I trade anything during Stage 1?** No. Everything here is paper. Real size arrives at Stage 5, after four gates.
3. **Is Gexbot part of the loop?** As context for the regime paid and the falsifier in leftover. It is no longer what you look at first; Chapter 12 said that too.
