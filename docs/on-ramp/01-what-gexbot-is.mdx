# Your footprint on the map

If you buy one index option this morning, you leave traces on the screens Chapter 1 mapped. None of them says it was you, and none of them says why.

**What this page assumes.** [Chapter 1](/docs/plans/01-what-gexbot-is): the five screens, open interest, volume, leftover, and the rule that a pile shows what is there and not who owns it. The [two seats](/docs/on-ramp/00-intro). **What you will be able to do.** Say where one trade of yours appears on each screen, when, and what each screen cannot know about it.

## Three traces from one trade

Buy one option contract on the index. Three things happen to the map, on three clocks.

At once, your contract is in today’s **volume** at that strike. Classic will draw it tonight as part of today’s footprints; until then it is one contract among the day’s count.

At once, if you were the aggressive side and no one traded the opposite way at the same strike to cancel you, your contract is in the **leftover** that State, Orderflow, and Quant draw. If someone did cancel you, your trade is in volume and nowhere else.

Tonight, if you still hold the contract, it is in **open interest**, and Classic’s slow map carries it tomorrow morning.

This book calls the set of traces one trade leaves across the screens its **footprint**.

## What the map cannot say about you

The map cannot say the trace is yours. It cannot say whether you opened a new position or closed an old one; Chapter 3 gives that limit its name. It cannot say whether you did something else at the same time that cancels the risk your contract carries.

Those limits split the screens. After a cancel, you are still in **volume** and gone from **leftover**. Classic tonight can still draw you; State now cannot. A reader who treats those two pictures as two views of the same trade has counted a cancelled contract as if it were still live. You are now on the far side of “we can see the pile, not who owns it,” and the sentence is as true of you as of anyone.

## The one thing that changes

When you trade ES, your fill lands on another futures trader with the same instrument and no obligation to you afterwards. When you buy an option, your fill lands on a firm that now has to do something in ES *because of you*, and that something is the force this whole book is about. Chapter 3 names the firm and what it must do. Chapter 12 calls this moment “you become the print.”

## Summary

A reader at 10:05 and a reader at tomorrow’s open are not looking at the same object when they look at this trade. The first can see you in volume and maybe in leftover. The second sees you only if you held. Neither can name you. The clocks are why “your bar” is not one bar.

## Newcomer questions

1. **You flatten before the close. Which traces die tonight, and which never become open interest?** Leftover dies when you sell (if it was yours and uncancelled). Volume keeps the two prints. Open interest never lists you; you did not hold through the snapshot.
2. **Does buying an option move ES?** Chapter 3 answers that. This page only says: your fill obliges someone; a futures fill does not.
3. **If someone sold the same strike a second later, where am I?** In volume. Not in leftover. The two of you cancelled, and the map is honest about that.
