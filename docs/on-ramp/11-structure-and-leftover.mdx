# Choosing your strike from structure

Structure tells a futures trader where price is and what it did. For a holder it decides which strike is theirs, and that decision is most of what a position is.

**What this page assumes.** [Chapter 11](/docs/practice/11-structure-and-leftover): the structure field, S1–S10, multi-leg structures, the hedged holder, the partial picture, and the rookie list. [When you are the level](/docs/on-ramp/08-heuristics-as-reading). **What you will be able to do.** Say how structure and leftover together describe a strike, and see your own legs the way a Gexbot reader sees them.

This page describes what a strike is once chosen. It does not say which to choose.

## Strike selection

Chapter 11’s combined setups put a structural location next to a Gexbot object and named the pressure that resulted. A holder makes the same combination in reverse: the strike they hold sits somewhere in the structure field and somewhere on the ladders, and those two facts describe the position before anything else does. Choosing which strike a position sits at, from where structure and leftover sit, is **strike selection**.

<TermFirst id="strike-selection" />

Two descriptions, both from Chapter 11, both about you if the strike is yours:

- A strike inside customer-long leftover at a swing high is in the wall of S2. Hold a long call there and you are among the holders whose incentive to sell on approach the sweep will test.
- A strike at a crowded short-convexity node is where the shove of S8 lands. Hold a short call there and the dealers’ chase through the node is a chase through your position.

Neither sentence recommends either strike. Both say what a holder there is.

## Your legs on the screen

Chapter 11 said a multi-leg trade prints two or more bars and that a Gexbot reader may read each leg as a separate opinion. When the legs are yours, you are the one being misread: a bought call and a sold call at two strikes are one position with one thesis, and the ladder shows two bars with two signs. The hedged-holder case is you too: if you sold ES against your calls, your Gexbot bar is drawn as if you had not, and the partial picture means that any position you hold in a venue Gexbot does not see leaves no bar at all.

## The one thing that changes

A futures trader has one leg. A holder may have several, and Gexbot reads each one on its own, so the screen you learned to read will describe your position as a set of parts and never as the whole. Chapter 14’s Gap 4 is where combinations are taught; its gate asks you to say what a Gexbot user would misread about you.

## Summary

Structure picks the shelf. Gexbot draws each leg on that shelf as if the leg were the whole position. The holder is the only one who can add the legs back up. A reader who names two opinions from your two bars is not wrong about the bars; they are wrong about you.

## Newcomer questions

1. **Does structure tell me which strike to buy?** No. It tells you what a holder at a given strike is, in the same words Chapter 11 used for the crowd.
2. **My two-leg position shows as two bars. Is Gexbot wrong?** No. It sees legs. It does not see positions.
3. **I hedged my calls with ES. Where is that on Gexbot?** Nowhere. Your bar is drawn as an unhedged directional customer; that is the inferred limit Chapter 3 named.
