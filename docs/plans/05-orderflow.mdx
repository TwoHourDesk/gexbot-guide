# Orderflow

**Which Gexbot screen this is.** Orderflow — the same leftover you learned to locate on State, plotted as time passes.

**What you will be able to do.** Tell a bar (“what just changed”) from a running score (“what is leftover so far today”), name a print with two signs, and treat the bar as the moment leftover pressure was created.

**What you already need.** [State](/docs/plans/04-state): DEX as a record, convexity as pending gamma, GEX profile as which wing. You also need Chapter 3’s hedge chain: an Orderflow bar *is* link 1, the print.

## Three pictures of the same leftover

State told you where the leftover sits. It cannot tell you when it arrived, and the moment it arrived is the moment someone was handed risk. Orderflow is that clock.

State’s ladders are a **stock**: leftover by strike, so far today.

An Orderflow **bar** is an **increment**: what just changed. That is the print.

A **net** (a running score) is the **integral**: leftover so far today, added up through time. Nets start empty at the cash open. Using a 15:50 ET net to explain a 09:35 fill is a **labeling leak**.

This book calls that trio **ladder versus increment versus net**. All three are residual. None of them is CME futures volume.

The bar is hedge-chain **link 1**. The net is the day’s lean of leftover — including pending gamma re-hedge — not a forecast.

## Increment of leftover delta: DEX orderflow

Take the DEX ladder from State. Now plot *how that leftover delta just changed*.

A bar up means customers just got **longer** in share-equivalent. A bar down means they just got **shorter**. Gexbot’s name is **DEX orderflow**.

This is still a **record** of a lean, not buying still to come. The professional who absorbed is doing link 3 *now*.

## Increment of which wing: GEX orderflow

Take the GEX profile. Plot how call-sided leftover gamma minus put-sided leftover gamma just changed.

A bar up means **more call-sided leftover**, not a buy signal. Gexbot’s name is **GEX orderflow**.

On SPX, Gexbot’s own docs often read a standout plus bar as call *selling* — a local-top *candidate* (*folklore*). That sentence is instrument-specific. It is not “bullish gamma.”

## Increment of paid versus collected: convexity orderflow

Take the convexity ladder. Plot how leftover option *buying* minus leftover option *selling* just changed.

A bar up is leftover **paid** gamma (vol bid). A bar down is leftover **collected** gamma (vol offer). Gexbot’s name is **convexity orderflow**.

This meter has no up or down for the *index*. Persistent plus-convexity often prints on selloffs; persistent minus-convexity on quiet grinds (*folklore*). Pair it with DEX to recover the cell below.

## Two signs name the print: the 2×2

DEX gives you a sign. Convexity gives you a second sign. Together they name four leftover prints:

|  | Convexity plus (paid) | Convexity minus (collected) |
|---|---|---|
| **DEX plus** | **Long call** — paid for upside **expansion** | **Short put** — collected; wants a **pin** above the strike |
| **DEX minus** | **Long put** — paid for downside expansion | **Short call** — collected on a call |

Same plus-DEX, opposite story. That is why this book refuses to act on DEX alone.

This book calls that table the **2×2**.

## Running scores (nets)

Add DEX orderflow through the day and you get **aggregate DEX**: share-equivalents leftover so far today. It can be split call versus put.

Add GEX orderflow and you get **net GEX**: running call-sided leftover gamma minus put-sided leftover gamma.

Add convexity orderflow and you get **net convexity**: leftover option buying minus leftover option selling so far today.

None of these nets existed at 09:30:01. They are so-far maps. A reader who explains a 09:35 fill with a 15:50 net has used a number that did not exist at the fill.

## Spike, sequence, noise

One large bar is an **event**: something just printed. Many small bars of the same sign are a **sequence**: they name a **day-type** (fear versus grind) only after you have enough of them. Alternating tiny bars are two-way cancel — **noise**. Pressure: **none**; nobody is left holding anything.

One large bar at 09:35 is a print, not a day. Name the day from it and you will read every node for the next hour through a lens that one print chose for you.

## Running example

**Bought 500 ATM calls at 10:05.** Plus DEX, plus convexity, plus GEX orderflow → the 2×2 names **long call**. Not “bullish”: paid convexity with a dealer who chases if the index walks up.

**Sold 500 ATM calls.** Minus DEX, minus convexity, **plus GEX orderflow** → **short call**. Same plus GEX-orderflow bar, opposite cell. That is why GEX orderflow is not a buy signal.

## Summary

A plus GEX-orderflow bar sits in both running-example cells. The 2×2 is the only name that splits them; the bar alone cannot. The net is a third object: leftover so far today, empty at the open, and a labeling leak if it is used to explain a morning fill.

## Newcomer questions

1. **Plus GEX orderflow means buy?** No. It means call-sided leftover grew. On SPX that is often selling (*folklore*).
2. **One spike is the day-type?** No. Sequence names the day; a spike marks a moment.
3. **Can I use the 15:50 net on a 09:35 fill?** Labeling leak.
