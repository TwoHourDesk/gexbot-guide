# State — leftover by strike

By the time a leftover lean appears on State, the first futures hedge that lean required is mostly already done — you cannot pick it out of the tape. What is still coming depends on where the index walks next, and this screen shows where, not whether.

**Which Gexbot screen this is.** State — leftover trades so far today, by strike, labeled customer bought versus customer sold.

**What you will be able to do.** Read four drawings without mixing their left-and-right meanings, and split a hedge that is **already done** from a hedge that is still **ahead** if the index walks.

**What you already need.** [Classification](/docs/plans/03-classification): residual, customer long versus short, share-equivalent, mandate versus incentive, and the six-link hedge chain. Delta’s S is [Chapter 2](/docs/plans/02-classic).

State starts empty at 09:30 ET and rewrites during cash regular hours. It is today’s leftover, not last night’s open interest.

## Leftover contracts by strike: the options profile

The first drawing is the simplest. At each strike, on each kind of option (call or put — this book says **per right**), Gexbot stacks how many leftover contracts are customer long versus customer short.

Long leftover is drawn to the **right**. Short leftover is drawn to the **left**. Puts are usually purple. Calls are usually orange.

This book, and Gexbot’s docs, call that picture the **options profile**, or **OP**.

It only draws **today’s expiry** and the **following expiry**. It does not draw a ninety-day mix. Chapter 6 will name those three groups. Until then: OP is a near-dated leftover count, not Classic’s open-interest warehouse.

## Implied volatility, walls, and fuel

The price of an option embeds a guess about how much the index will wiggle. That guess is **implied volatility**, often written **IV**.

Through a session, that guess can generally be falling (a **crush**) or rising. This book calls that the **vol regime**. It matters because the holder of a leftover option is winning or losing depending on which way that guess moves, and their incentive at the strike flips with it: the same bar can argue for a stall *candidate* in one regime and for a continuation *candidate* in the other. If you cannot tell which regime you are in, stand down on any “wall versus fuel” story.

Gexbot’s own docs tell a *folklore* story that **flips** with the regime:

In a **falling-vol** tape, customer **longs** are said to act like **walls**: holders are said to sell the option on approach and supply liquidity — a stall *candidate*. Customer **shorts** are said to act like **fuel**: holders are said to hedge or add and take liquidity — a continuation *candidate*.

In a **rising-vol** tape, both stories invert. A long leftover can become fuel. A short leftover can become a wall.

A wall is a **hypothesis**, not a fact. A reader who treats a tall long bar as support, and leans on it without a regime, is using a falling-vol story on a tape that may have already flipped. “Long bar = support” is the first import mistake on this page.

## Which wing is heavy: the GEX profile

The second drawing keeps leftover, but now weights it by **gamma** and nets leftover *calls* against leftover *puts* at each strike.

Right means the **call wing** of the leftover is heavier. Left means the **put wing** is heavier. That is **not** bought versus sold. Two huge leftover books, one call and one put, can net toward zero here the same way Classic’s unsigned bars can.

This book calls that picture the **GEX profile**. It looks like Classic’s histogram. It is not Classic: the bars are classified leftover, not open interest or raw volume.

## Leftover share-equivalent: the DEX ladder

Chapter 2 taught the S of delta; Chapter 3 applied it to leftover as share-equivalent. State now plots leftover **delta** at each strike.

Customer long calls and customer short puts both lean the same way: they are **plus** leftover share-equivalent. Customer short calls and customer long puts are **minus**.

Gexbot’s name is **DEX** — delta exposure — drawn as a **DEX ladder**.

## Already done versus still ahead

This is the hinge of State.

Go back to 10:05. The customer lifted 500 calls; within seconds, the dealer bought about 500 ES. You did not see that print and cannot find it on your tape, but by the time the plus-DEX bar is on your screen, that buying has happened. A reader who sees the bar at 10:06 and buys ES expecting 500 contracts of dealer demand is buying after the demand, not ahead of it. So: DEX is a **record** of a lean — hedge-chain **link 3**, mostly **already on**. It is not a queue of buying still to come. “DEX bar = pending buying” is the second import mistake on this page.

What is still **ahead** is hedge-chain **link 4**: if the index walks, gamma changes delta, and the professional must top up or unwind. That pending force is **path-conditional**. It appears only if the index walks a certain way. You now have the record. The next drawing is the part still owed: where, and with which sign. It cannot show the path.

## Leftover gamma, bought minus sold: convexity

The fourth drawing weights leftover by **gamma**, then signs it **long option minus short option**.

Paid leftover (long calls and long puts) is **plus**. Collected leftover (short calls and short puts) is **minus**.

Gexbot’s name is **convexity**. They chose the word to stress **vol ownership**, not up or down. Convexity has no bullish or bearish. Plus means leftover paper that *wants a larger move than priced*. Minus means leftover paper that *wants a smaller move*.

## Running example

**Bought 500 ATM calls.** Options profile: long-call bar to the **right**. DEX: **plus**. Convexity: **plus** at the strike. Customer **incentivized** (theta, falling vol) to sell on approach (*folklore*). Dealer **mandated** to **chase** if the index rips — pending gamma, link 4.

**Sold 500 ATM calls.** Options profile: short-call bar to the **left**. DEX: **minus**. Convexity: **minus**. Customer incentivized to sit or defend. Dealer mandated to **fade**.

DEX is the record of the about-500 ES already done (link 3). Convexity is the about-300 ES that appear only if the index walks +15 (link 4).

## Summary

The two import mistakes on this page fail at different times. “Long bar = support” fails when the regime is wrong. “DEX = pending buying” fails even when the regime is right, because the clock for that hedge has already passed. Left on the options profile and left on the GEX profile fail for a third reason: they are different axes, not a color error.

## Newcomer questions

1. **Same long bar, rising vol. Wall or fuel, and which pressure word?** Fuel *candidate*, still incentive, still folklore. The falling-vol map does not travel. If you cannot tag the regime, refuse the wall.
2. **Plus-DEX printed at 10:06. You buy ES at 10:06 expecting the 500.** Who already did that trade? The dealer, within seconds of 10:05. The bar is the record. The 500 you wanted were the ones you did not see.
3. **Is GEX profile the same as convexity?** No. Wing (call versus put) versus ownership (paid versus collected).
